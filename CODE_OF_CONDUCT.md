# Code of Conduct

This document is short on purpose. A long code of conduct invites lawyering; a short one is
enforced on judgement, and the judgement is accountable because every enforcement action is
recorded.

## The standard

Participate in good faith. Argue with the idea, not the person. Assume the other party is
competent and reading carefully until they prove otherwise.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, threats, or sustained hostility toward a person.
- Demeaning anyone for who they are rather than what they argued.
- Deliberate derailing: repeating a settled point to exhaust the other side, brigading a
  thread, or arguing in volume rather than substance.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Bad-faith participation: sockpuppets, manufactured consensus, or engaging only to waste
  reviewers' time.

## Explicitly acceptable

- Blunt technical criticism. "This design is broken and here is the attack" is a contribution,
  not an insult.
- Disagreeing with a maintainer, publicly and at length, on the merits.
- Being wrong. Nobody is penalised for a bad idea offered honestly.
- Refusing to accept an argument from authority.

The line is behaviour toward people, never the content of a technical or governance position.
A Code of Conduct that can be used to end a technical disagreement is a capture tool, and this
one MUST NOT be used that way. Anyone invoking it to win an argument on the merits is misusing
it, and that misuse is itself a violation.

## Enforcement

Report to **conduct@webx.dev**. Reports are handled by the maintainers, excluding any
maintainer who is a party to the report.

Responses, in escalating order: a private word; a public correction; a temporary suspension
from project spaces; a permanent one. Nothing beyond a private word happens without a written
rationale, and the rationale is published — redacted only to protect a reporter's identity.

Enforcement is recorded and auditable. A project whose moderation cannot be reviewed has
recreated exactly the unaccountable power WebX exists to remove.

## Appeal

Any action may be appealed once, in writing, to the maintainers as a group. If the appeal is
refused, the appellant may publish their appeal and the refusal, and the project will not
retaliate for that publication.

## Scope

This repository, its issue tracker, its Radicle mirror, and any space the project runs under
its own name. It does not extend to participants' unrelated conduct elsewhere.
