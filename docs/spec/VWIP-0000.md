# VWIP-0000: The VayuWeb Improvement Proposal Process

```text
VWIP:      0000
Title:     The VayuWeb Improvement Proposal Process
Author:    Ankush Choudhary Johal <ankushchoudharyjohal@gmail.com>
Status:    Final
Type:      Process
Category:  --
Created:   2026-07-26
Requires:  --
Replaces:  --
```

## Abstract

This document defines how VayuWeb changes. Every alteration to the protocol, the namespace policy,
the conformance suite or the process itself moves through a numbered VayuWeb Improvement Proposal
(VWIP) with a named author, a stated rationale, a recorded set of objections, and a permanent
public archive.

VWIP-0000 is the meta-proposal: it specifies the format, the lifecycle, the mandatory sections
and the consensus rule. It operationalises Title V of the
[Constitution](../../constitution/CONSTITUTION.md); where this document and the Constitution
diverge, the Constitution governs and this document is defective.

**Status:** Draft against the pre-implementation design. The process is in force from
publication; the protocol it governs has not been built yet.

This is a narrower claim than it may read as, and section 2.4.b turns on the difference. *This
document* binds from publication, because a process that waited for the thing it governs would
have nothing to govern the drafting of it. *The Constitution* is a separate question: Article
60.3 makes its commencement conditional on publishing the anchored canonical text alongside the
first conformance suite and the first VWIP archive, and the anchor has not been published. So
the charter governs this work as the drafters' own commitment, and it is not yet in force in the
sense Article 60.3 means.

## Motivation

Two failure modes kill long-lived protocols, and they are opposites.

The first is **ossification**: nothing can change, because no path exists to change it, so the
protocol calcifies around its first mistakes and is eventually replaced wholesale by something
worse but newer. The second is **churn**: anything can change, because whoever holds the
repository decides, so the specification becomes a description of last week's code and
independent implementation becomes impossible.

A written process is the only known defence against both. It makes change possible, which
defeats ossification, and it makes change *expensive and auditable*, which defeats churn. The
cost is deliberate: VayuWeb would rather lose a good idea to procedural friction than acquire a bad
one by acclamation.

There is a third motive, specific to this project. A reader in 2126 must be able to reconstruct
not only what was decided but *why*, and what was argued against it. Article 43.7 puts it
plainly: the Objection Register exists so that a reader in fifty years has the argument as well
as the outcome. A decision whose dissent went unrecorded cannot be audited, and an unauditable
governance record is precisely the condition VayuWeb exists to end.

## Specification

### 1. Header block

Every VWIP MUST open with a fenced `text` block carrying exactly these fields, in this order:

```text
VWIP:      0042
Title:     A short noun phrase, 60 characters or fewer
Author:    Name or persistent pseudonym <contact>
Status:    Draft | Review | Accepted | Implemented | Final | Deprecated | Retired
           | Rejected | Withdrawn | Dormant
Type:      Standards Track | Process | Informational | Constitutional Amendment
Category:  Core | Registry | Resolution | Hosting | Client | Interoperability
           | Security | Naming
Created:   YYYY-MM-DD
Requires:  comma-separated VWIP numbers, or --
Replaces:  comma-separated VWIP numbers, or --
```

1.1 `Category` MUST be `--` for Process and Informational proposals and MUST be present for
Standards Track and Constitutional Amendment proposals.

1.2 `Author` MAY be a pseudonym, but it MUST be persistent within the process, as required by
Article 43.3. A pseudonymous author has exactly the standing of a named one.

1.3 Numbers are assigned by an editor on entry to Draft, sequentially, and are never reused.

### 2. Types and categories

2.1 **Standards Track** proposals change something observable between independent
implementations: a wire format, a validation rule, a resolution behaviour, a namespace policy.
These are the only proposals that require two independent implementations before Final.

2.2 **Process** proposals change how the project decides, including this document.

2.3 **Informational** proposals record guidance, analysis or a design rationale. They bind
nobody. An Informational VWIP MUST NOT contain normative RFC 2119 keywords describing VayuWeb
behaviour, because the temptation to smuggle a requirement into an advisory document is
perennial.

2.4 **Constitutional Amendment** proposals alter the text of the Constitution. They are the only
instrument that can, and Article 41 makes the route exclusive: there is no editorial, emergency
or consensus path by which charter text changes without one. An amendment MUST satisfy Article
58.1 in full — full replacement text, rights-impact and capture analyses, twelve months of
deliberation with two readings six months apart, prospective-only effect, a concurrent
75 percent supermajority in each of the four Article 40 constituencies counted separately, and
double ratification on unchanged text six months apart.

2.4.a An amendment purporting to reach an entrenched clause of Article 9 is **inadmissible**,
not merely unlikely to pass. An editor MUST refuse it at the completeness check, and that
refusal is the one occasion on which an editor declines a proposal for a reason other than a
missing section — because Article 9.20 makes such an amendment void ab initio whatever the
process does with it, and running a twelve-month deliberation on a void instrument lends it a
legitimacy the charter denies it.

2.4.b Until commencement under Article 60.3, there is no charter in force to amend and no
constituency in which to count a supermajority. Changes to the text in that window are
**drafting**, not amendment, and Article 58 does not reach them. This distinction is
load-bearing and it is also self-extinguishing: it expires permanently the moment the canonical
digest is anchored. A drafting change made in that window MUST still be published as a
Constitutional Amendment VWIP carrying every mandatory section of section 3, so that the record
a reader inherits is identical in form to the one an amendment would have left. What differs is
the ratification arithmetic, which nobody could have performed, and not the reasoning, which
everybody can audit. An implementer who wants a change and finds this window closed has an
answer they may dislike: wait twelve months, or fork.

2.5 Standards Track and Constitutional Amendment categories are `Core`, `Registry`,
`Resolution`, `Hosting`, `Client`, `Interoperability`, `Security` and `Naming`. The category
determines the minimum Review duration under section 4.

2.6 **Naming** is the category reserved by Article 35.6 for bringing a top-level domain into
being, and is the only category that can. A Naming proposal carries three requirements no other
category does, all from Articles 35.6 and 35.7: a collision review against existing VayuWeb
extensions and against legacy DNS strings likely to confuse a user, a public objection window of
not less than ninety days, and a dormancy of not less than one hundred and eighty days between
ratification and availability, with the activation epoch published at least that far ahead so
that advance knowledge confers no landrush advantage. A Naming VWIP that omits the collision
review is incomplete under section 3 and MUST NOT leave Draft.

### 3. Mandatory sections

Per Article 42.4, a VWIP MUST NOT advance beyond Draft unless it contains all of the following.
An editor checks their presence, never their merit.

| Section | What it must do |
|---|---|
| Abstract | State the change in a paragraph a non-specialist can follow. |
| Motivation | State the problem *before* the solution. A motivation that only describes the proposed mechanism is incomplete. |
| Specification | Normative text using RFC 2119 keywords, per Article 3, correctly and consistently. |
| Security considerations | Mandatory. MUST NOT be "none". |
| Privacy considerations | Mandatory. MUST NOT be "none". |
| Rights-impact analysis | Name every Title II Article the change touches and state, for each, whether the effect is expansion, restriction or none. |
| Impossibility-and-capture analysis | Required by Article 5: what does this make impossible, and what new thing does it make worth capturing? |
| Centralisation analysis | Required by Article 4: identify every party whose withdrawal would degrade the mechanism. If the list is not empty, justify each entry. |
| Migration and rollback | How deployments move, and how they move back if the change proves wrong. |
| Activation epoch | Strictly in the future, per Article 47. |
| Expiry of transitional mechanisms | Every temporary bridge gets a date, per Article 48. |
| Test vectors | Required for anything observable on the wire. |
| Collision review | **Naming only.** Required by Article 35.6: every proposed extension checked against the ratified set and against legacy DNS strings likely to confuse a user, with the result stated for each rather than summarised. |
| Full replacement text | **Constitutional Amendment only.** Required by Article 58.1.a: the amended Article reproduced in full, not a description of the edit. |

3.1 "None" is not an acceptable answer to the security or privacy sections. Where a change
genuinely has no security consequence, the author MUST explain *why* — that explanation is the
reviewable artefact, and writing it is where authors most often discover that there is a
consequence after all.

3.2 The centralisation analysis is the section most often written dishonestly, because almost
every convenience concentrates something. An analysis that identifies no dependent party at all
SHOULD be treated by reviewers as an unfinished analysis rather than a clean result.

### 4. Lifecycle

Per Article 42.1, the lifecycle is:

```text
   ┌─────────┐   30d min   ┌────────┐   consensus   ┌──────────┐
   │  Draft  ├────────────►│ Review ├──────────────►│ Accepted │
   └────┬────┘             └───┬────┘               └────┬─────┘
        │                      │                         │ deployed
        │                      │ objection sustained     ▼
        │                      │                    ┌─────────────┐
        │                      └───────────────────►│ Implemented │
        │                             │             └──────┬──────┘
        │                             ▼                    │ 2 independent impls
        │                       ┌──────────┐               ▼
        │                       │ Rejected │          ┌───────┐
        │                       └──────────┘          │ Final │
        │                                             └───┬───┘
        │ author only          ┌───────────┐              │
        ├─────────────────────►│ Withdrawn │              ▼
        │                      └───────────┘        ┌────────────┐
        │ 365d inactivity      ┌─────────┐          │ Deprecated │
        └─────────────────────►│ Dormant │          └─────┬──────┘
                               └─────────┘                ▼
                                                     ┌──────────┐
                                                     │ Retired  │
                                                     └──────────┘
```

4.1 **Minimum durations** (Article 42.5):

| Transition | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Draft | 30 days |
| Review — most categories | 90 days |
| Review — Core, Registry, Security | 180 days |
| Review — Naming | 90 days objection window (Art. 35.6), then 180 days dormancy before availability (Art. 35.7) |
| Review — Constitutional Amendment | 12 months, two readings at least 6 months apart (Art. 58.1.b) |
| Accepted → Implemented | none |
| Implemented → Final | set by Article 42.5 and not shortened by agreement |

4.1.a A Naming proposal's ninety-day objection window and its hundred-and-eighty-day dormancy
are consecutive, not concurrent, and the dormancy runs from ratification rather than from
acceptance. The shortest possible path from a Naming Draft to a registrable extension is
therefore 30 + 90 + 180 days. Nothing shortens it, including unanimity: the dormancy exists
precisely so that the people closest to the decision gain no head start, and a dormancy waived
by the people it constrains would not be one.

4.2 Minimums are floors, not targets. An editor MUST NOT shorten one because a proposal is
popular, urgent-sounding, or authored by a maintainer. Article 57 governs genuine emergencies and
carries an automatic sunset; nothing else does.

4.3 **Dormant** is entered automatically after 365 days without substantive activity. A Dormant
VWIP MAY be reopened by anyone, including someone other than its author (Article 42.3).

4.4 **No VWIP in any state is ever deleted** (Article 42.3). Rejected and Withdrawn proposals
stay in the archive permanently, with their reasons. The record of what was refused, and why, is
as valuable as the record of what was adopted — and far more likely to be needed by whoever
proposes the same thing again in twenty years.

### 5. Rough consensus

5.1 Consensus means **the absence of unaddressed substantive technical objection**. It is not
unanimity, a head count, a majority, or the preference of whoever is chairing (Article 43.1).

5.2 An objection MUST be specific, technical and publicly reasoned; MUST identify the harm it
asserts; SHOULD propose a remedy; and MUST be attributable to a persistent participant identity,
which MAY be a pseudonym (Article 43.3).

5.3 Every objection MUST be answered on the record. **Answered does not mean accommodated** — a
reasoned refusal to change the proposal is a complete answer (Article 43.2).

5.4 The following do **not** constitute consensus:

- A vote count. Fifty people agreeing does not answer one correct objection.
- Volume or repetition. A swarm of accounts is not a community.
- Seniority or tenure. "I have been here longest" is not an argument.
- Silence from people who never read the proposal.

5.5 The following do **not** constitute a blocking objection:

- Preference, taste, affiliation or timing alone. These SHALL be disregarded, and the disregard
  SHALL be recorded with its reasoning. Silent dismissal is prohibited (Article 43.4).
- An objection already answered and not renewed with new substance within 90 days, which is
  spent for blocking purposes but remains in the record (Article 43.5).

5.6 Every unresolved objection SHALL be entered permanently in a public **Objection Register**
attached to the VWIP, together with the reasoning by which it was overruled and the identity of
whoever recorded that reasoning (Article 43.6). The Register is what converts "rough consensus"
from a euphemism for discretion into a reviewable act.

5.7 No participant, however long-serving, may block indefinitely without renewed substance.
Repeated blocking without new substance is itself recorded (Article 43.8).

### 6. Running code

6.1 No Standards Track VWIP reaches Final without running code (Article 44.1).

6.2 The minimum is **two independent, interoperating implementations**, written by parties with
no common employer, funder or controlling entity, each passing the published test vectors
(Article 44.2).

6.3 A change that cannot attract a second independent implementation is evidence of a
single-vendor protocol and MUST NOT advance, however elegant its specification (Article 44.3).

6.4 A reference implementation MAY exist for clarity. **It is not normative** (Article 44.4).
Where an implementation and the specification disagree, the specification governs and the
implementation is defective. Where the specification is genuinely ambiguous, the remedy is a
clarifying VWIP — never deference to whichever code is most widely deployed (Article 44.5).

6.5 Specification text MUST be sufficient to build a conformant client **without reading any
implementation's source**. Failure of this property is a defect in the specification and grounds
for returning the VWIP to Review (Article 44.6).

### 7. Editors

7.1 Editing is a **ministerial function**. Editors check completeness, assign numbers, record
state transitions, maintain the archive and publish the record (Article 46.1).

7.2 Editors have **no merit veto**, no casting vote, and no authority over names, registry state,
resolution, conformance results, or anyone else's software (Article 46.2). An editor who rejects
a proposal because they disagree with it has exceeded their role, and that is itself grounds for
removal.

7.3 Refusal to publish a VWIP is appealable on the record and MUST be answered within 30 days. A
VWIP deadlocked among editors goes to extended review, never to an editor's decision
(Article 46.3).

7.4 The panel SHALL be **not fewer than three persons** and MUST NOT be held by one person at any
time. Where it falls below three, editorial transitions are suspended until it is refilled
(Article 46.4). Terms are two years, staggered so no more than one seat turns over in any
eight-month period, limited to two consecutive terms (Article 46.5).

7.5 An editor MUST recuse from any VWIP in which they, their employer or their funder has an
interest, and every recusal is entered in a public recusal register (Article 46.6).

7.6 No single employer, funder or commonly controlled group may hold more than one third of the
panel (Article 46.7).

7.7 An editor unreachable for 120 consecutive days vacates the seat automatically. This dead-man
rule also protects pseudonymous holders who must disappear without explanation, and no
explanation is required of them (Article 46.8).

## Security considerations

The process itself is an attack surface, and historically the most productive one — capturing a
standards process is cheaper than breaking a cipher.

**Editor capture.** Countered by the three-person minimum, staggered fixed terms, the one-third
employer cap, mandatory recusal, and the strict ministerial limit on editorial power. An editor
who cannot veto on merit is not worth capturing.

**Consensus manufacture.** Countered by section 5.4: head counts, sockpuppet volume and seniority
are all explicitly excluded from what constitutes consensus. Because consensus is defined
negatively — absence of unaddressed objection — adding supporters does not create it.

**Objection suppression.** Countered by the Objection Register and the prohibition on silent
dismissal. An objection can be overruled but cannot be erased, and the overruling is signed.

**Fast-tracking under manufactured urgency.** Countered by section 4.2 and the fixed minimum
durations. Genuine emergencies route through Article 57, which carries an automatic sunset.

**Specification drift toward one implementation.** Countered by section 6.5 and Article 44.6: if
a conformant client cannot be built from the text alone, the text is defective.

**Residual risk.** A sufficiently patient adversary who supplies most of the review labour
influences outcomes without ever violating a rule. No process defeats this. The remedies are
external: the annual implementation-diversity report (Article 44.9), the concentration metrics of
Article 53, and ultimately Article 59 — declaration of capture and the right of fork.

## Privacy considerations

Participation MAY be pseudonymous at every stage, including authorship and editorship. A
persistent pseudonym has exactly the standing of a legal name; nothing in this process requires
identity verification, and no mechanism to require it may be added without amending Article 43.

The archive is permanent and public. Anyone contributing under a pseudonym should understand that
writing style, timing and technical fingerprints are correlatable across a decades-long record —
this document cannot fix that, and does not pretend to.

The 120-day dead-man rule of section 7.7 exists partly so that a pseudonymous editor who must
vanish can do so without ever explaining why.

## Rights-impact analysis

| Title II Article | Effect |
|---|---|
| Article 16 — right to interoperate and implement independently | **Expansion.** Sections 6.2–6.5 make independent implementation a precondition of finality rather than an afterthought. |
| Article 17 — right to fork | **None.** Nothing here conditions, delays or penalises a fork. |
| Article 20 — freedom from retroactive rule changes | **Expansion.** The mandatory future activation epoch (section 3) forbids retroactive effect by construction. |

No Title II right is restricted by this document.

## Impossibility-and-capture analysis

**What this makes impossible.** A silent change. Every alteration leaves a numbered, permanent,
publicly archived record carrying its dissent. There is no mechanism by which a change can take
effect without one, and no editorial power capable of creating such a mechanism.

**What this makes worth capturing.** The editor panel — which is why its powers are stripped to
clerical acts and its composition is constrained six different ways. The residual prize is
influence over the *pace* of change, not its direction. That is a real prize, and section 4.2
plus the fixed minimums are the only defence offered; the honest position is that it is a partial
one.

## Centralisation analysis

Required by Article 4: every party whose withdrawal would degrade the mechanism.

**The editor panel.** If every editor stopped acting, no proposal could reach Accepted, because
the completeness check and the archive publication are editorial acts. This is the one genuine
dependency, and it is why section 7 strips the role to clerical work, constrains composition six
ways, and makes an editor's refusal appealable. A panel that cannot decide anything is a panel
whose withdrawal costs a delay rather than a direction.

**The archive.** Section 4 requires every proposal, including rejected and withdrawn ones, to
remain publicly retrievable. A single host of that archive would be a dependency, so the archive
is required to be reproducible from the repository rather than served from one place. Withdrawal
of any one mirror degrades convenience, not availability.

**Nobody else.** Ratification is rough consensus under Article 43, which is the absence of
unaddressed substantive technical objection rather than a count — so there is no constituency
whose non-participation blocks a proposal, and no role whose absence stops the process. That is
the property this document exists to preserve, and it is also the reason the analysis is short:
almost every convenience that would shorten it would concentrate something.

This section was itself missing from this document for a period, while the same document made it
mandatory for every other proposal. Section 3.2's warning applies to that omission more sharply
than to any thin analysis: an absent centralisation analysis is not a clean result.

## Migration and rollback

**Migration.** There is nothing to migrate from. No prior process existed, no proposal was ever
accepted under another one, and no document needs rewriting to conform. A repository adopting
VayuWeb adopts this process with the corpus.

**Rollback.** Replacing this process means a Process VWIP that supersedes VWIP-0000, carrying
every section this table requires, and it faces one asymmetry worth stating: a proposal to change
the amendment process is judged under the process it proposes to change. Article 41 makes the
VWIP route exclusive, so there is no path by which a superseding process takes effect without
going through this one first. That is the intended shape, and it means rollback is slow by
construction rather than by oversight.

Proposals already Final under this process remain Final if it is superseded. A process change is
prospective, per Article 47.3, and retroactively invalidating ratified proposals would make every
Final status conditional on the process never changing again.

## Activation epoch

**On publication.** This document is Process rather than Standards Track, and Article 47's
activation floor governs changes that alter what peers put on the wire. A process document alters
what proposals must contain, which binds authors and editors from the moment it is published and
binds no running software at all.

The distinction matters because Article 20.3's 180-day floor is expensive and specific: it exists
so that a peer following the old rules and a peer following the new ones cannot disagree about a
record. No record's validity depends on this document, so applying the floor here would delay a
document nothing can be broken by.

**Until commencement, this is drafting rather than amendment.** Article 60.3 makes commencement
conditional on the anchored canonical text, which is not published, so section 2.4.b applies and
the activation question for anything touching charter text is answered there rather than here.

## Expiry of transitional mechanisms

Required by Article 48: every temporary bridge gets a date. This document defines **one**.

**Section 2.4.b — the drafting window.** Until commencement under Article 60.3, changes to charter
text are drafting rather than amendment, and Article 58 does not reach them. The mechanism is
self-extinguishing rather than dated, and deliberately so: it expires permanently the moment the
canonical digest is anchored, which is an event no party can postpone by inaction, since Article
60.3 makes commencement conditional on it. A date would be worse — it would either expire the
window before the anchor exists, leaving the corpus unamendable, or outlive the anchor and leave
a live bypass of Article 58.

Everything else here is permanent. The lifecycle, the mandatory sections, the editor constraints
and the archive requirement carry no sunset, because none of them is a bridge from one state to
another.

## Test vectors

Required for anything observable on the wire. **Nothing in this document is**, and the reason is
worth writing rather than asserting: a VWIP's header block, its section list and its status
transitions are read by people and by `scripts/check-vwips.py`, never by a peer deciding whether
to accept a record. No implementation disagrees with another about a proposal.

What exists instead is a mechanical check, which is what section 3's "an editor checks their
presence, never their merit" describes. `scripts/check-vwips.py` reads the mandatory-sections
table out of this document — rather than restating it — and fails if any VWIP beyond Draft is
missing a section its Type or Category requires. It found this document missing five of its own,
while Final.

The two conditional rows are checked conditionally: Collision review only for `Category: Naming`,
Full replacement text only for `Type: Constitutional Amendment`. A check that demanded both from
every proposal would be failed by every proposal, and a check everyone learns to ignore enforces
nothing.

## Backwards compatibility

VWIP-0000 defines the process and has no predecessor to be compatible with. Proposals begin at
VWIP-0001.
